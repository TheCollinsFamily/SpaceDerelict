## 3. The index

Collins: after krypteia and testing, the survivors "enter the index (the wider imperial system)".

The corpus's Index (TPG 2.6) was a registry of **Houses**, each House a family culture with its
own recorded theology and practices, and a record of how its children fared, so that every House
could adopt what worked elsewhere. The central body ran what needs scale — marriage markets,
education — and "does not act as a theological governance organization" (HOME 1.0). Grown to an
empire, the Index is the state.

**PROPOSAL, how it works now:**
- **Entering the index** is becoming a legal adult: you get your own entry, with your House, your
  krypteia result, your test scores, and from then on your **standing**.
- **Standing** is the Index's running measure of a life's contribution to the potential of man.
  Service metrics feed it. It buys rank, resources, and eventually the procreation licence.
- **The Procreation Licensing Board** is the Index office that runs the marriage market. A licence
  admits you to it; the Board matches a spouse and assigns the womb allotment.
- **Houses.** A new couple stays in a parent House or founds a new one (TPG 2.6). Their children
  inherit the House record and are duty-bound to improve on it (HOME 1.0). Collins, Oct 14 2026: "to become
  a full Technopuritan you have to be accepted into one of the Houses, sub-governing structures, and that
  requires marriage"; a convert from outside has no House and so must marry into one.
- **No imperial church.** The Index governs goods, not belief. Theology lives in each House's
  record. That is how an authoritarian empire can also be doctrinally decentralised, and why
  imperial officials are so careful to say "the Index records" rather than "the Index believes".
- **The Office.** The branches of the Index that run things (the clearance Office, the Tender
  Office, Command) speak in forms. Every form has a line for "expected contribution".

## 5. Krypteia

Collins: at 13 the children go on krypteia, where they have to survive for themselves, living
among a subjugated population, for a year.

The corpus: of all rites for a rebellious youth, the Collinses found the Spartan krypteia "even
more compelling" than Rumspringa: youths hid among the helots and fed themselves by stealing
without being seen; they held a support network that shields people from their mistakes to be
"intrinsically evil", and that a teen who believes there is an out will not become an adult; they
would reject a self-sufficiency rite for one sex only (TPG 12.1). The Empire made it the rule.

- **Every child, sons and daughters,** at 13, for one year. No rescue, no recall, no out.
- **PROPOSAL, the subjugated:** the helot worlds hold two kinds of people: human polities that
  broke the covenant (they sought to subjugate others, so its protection lapsed: TP 11.0), and
  non-human intelligences kept "to serve" (TP 11.0). A youth lives among them, unseen, by their
  own means.
- **PROPOSAL, the record:** the Index records whether a youth survived the year, not how. The
  corpus hoped for a "non-murdery" version (TPG 12.1); the Empire's form has no box for the
  question.
- Only about a fifth of each cohort comes through krypteia and school together (Collins, Oct 14 2026; it was "about
  half" in his notes of Sep 30).

## 6. Testing and education

Collins, Oct 14 2026 (replacing his Sep 30 note that about half survive): "only about 20% of adolescents survive
through 'school', which goes up through what we consider a PhD and is a brutal series of tests to attempt to honestly
test 'fitness', under the belief that if this was not done people would either find ways to fake fitness and recreate
the dysgenic crisis of the 20th century (it's how they maintain 'fairness' of judging), and that if this did not happen
the most genetically successful people would just be the ones that bred the most, which is not a good metric of how
useful they are to God, e.g. the Empire; and it prevents nepotism, as it's generally higher status to take out students
with higher-status parents, who are presumably more fit. Given that you just come back in a simulation after being
killed, they struggle to see any cruelty in this and instead treat deferring the long-term thriving of humanity for
your own moral purity as the cruel thing to do." Students kill students, and it counts: the technician spaced a
classmate called Prime (YOKE's `back-12` line); the partner candidate's file records her kills, and "never by physical
violence" is a notable entry.

**PROPOSAL:** school runs from the end of krypteia, at about 14, to about 24, academic, physical and doctrinal, with
the survival camp (months alone, content/partner.ts) inside it; ranked, never passed or failed in words (the Index
records scores, and the scores decide the posting). A youth enters the index at about 24. The Broodfall technician is
a year or two past it.

## 6b. Nothing is forced: the moral record (Collins, Oct 14 2026)

Collins: "very little in the Empire is actually forced: going to this brutal school thing, nor is not raising your own
kids, nor is non-sexual reproduction (thus the cat people). It's just that you have 'moral' failings marked alongside
your social id, and these can be passed down to the next generation as well if bad enough, and have huge effects on
the types of jobs you can get. Not like you are going to starve if you don't have a good job; the society is totally
post-scarcity. But the very post-scarcity nature of society made people hungrier for status and work, as work confers
the true thing of scarcity in society, which is purpose. So people very rarely break from the society (as to them it
all makes sense)."

- So the school, the tanks, the Index raising the children, the hormonal treatment: each is the respectable choice, not
  the law. Refusing one is a mark on the record beside your social id; enough marks, and your children carry them.
- The partner candidate's "four censorships for working on the Sabbath" is such a mark (content/partner.ts); the cat
  girls, who breed sexually and keep their breasts, live inside the Empire with the marks that brings, or on the fringe.
- **PROPOSAL:** the record is public to the Board and to employers, which is why a form lists censorships and relatives;
  a mark is lived down by work, not paid off; the Index's standing is the positive side of the same ledger.
- **The exit** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): "even if someone did decide they don't care about living by the Index's rules,
  they simply would not stay in Technopuritan space." The rim, Space Derelict's setting (section 13), is what they go
  to: the other post-scarcity society, without aspiration to morality or purpose, with constant blood sports, piracy
  and the like. The two games are the two answers to post-scarcity: the Empire's, where purpose is the scarce thing
  and people compete for it; the rim's, where nothing is, and people fill it with spectacle.

## 7. Bodies

Collins: Technopuritans practise heavy genetic engineering and integration with AI. The player's
own body is only about **40% organic**, but the retro look — looking like an organic human — is in
fashion these days, which is why it is not obvious.

- Doctrine: improving human capacity by genetic and synthetic means is a mandate (TP 12.0), and
  the covenant accepts "genetically engineered specialists, human AI cyborgs, and human hive minds"
  as sons of man (TP 11.0).
- **PROPOSAL:** the fashion for the retro look is tolerated because it signals nothing (a status
  display would be a sin); a visibly chrome body would be showing off.

- **Bodies by world** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): humans who live on high-gravity planets are nothing like those optimised

- **Years are Sol years** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): the Empire counts age in Sol years, never "standard years"; a form says
  "23 Sol years" and nothing about apparent age, since everyone looks about that.
- **Same-sex attraction** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): generally dealt with through hormonal treatment and genetic
  modification. A person in whom part of it resists the treatment may judge themselves morally compromised; the
  companion of Broodfall's partner candidate did, and chose to leave "the meat world" (the living world, in slang) by

  The society's own argument, in the companion's mouth (Collins, Oct 14 2026): the genetic technology to reproduce

  create a more stable whole; not to be satisfied with the conditions and role of one's birth is deeply selfish and
  indulgent. She is fully bought in, and is disgusted and confused by anyone who suggests same-sex attraction is fine.
- **"The meat world"** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): slang for the living world, as against the simulations the dead live in.
- **Companions** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): most Technopuritans have one, what YOKE is to the technician: a synthetic
  intelligence made partially from the person's own nervous tissue. Having a dead person as one's companion instead is

- **The Lipptors** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): a class genetically specialised for advanced mathematics and extensive AI

## 7b. Ageing and euthanasia (Collins, Oct 12 2026)

Collins, Oct 14 2026: "most people without partners by 40 are pushed to ascend early ... essentially kill themselves."
To ascend is to be uploaded; early is before one's contribution is judged over. It is why Broodfall's partner
candidate's companion, who thinks the technician "just an exterminator" and beneath Atlas, needs the match to work all
the same: Atlas's eccentricities make a partner hard to secure (content/letters.ts).

Collins: in their society nobody ages past 23 (women); for men, when to stop ageing is more of an
aesthetic choice, usually made by the wife. Because it would be trivial to live forever, and that would
lead to societal stagnation, people are euthanised: when they feel their contribution is over; when
society has moved past their frame of reference (saying "kids these days are so..." is likely to get
"think it's time for euthanasia, grandpa"); or at a strictly enforced cap of **120 years**, from God's
cap at that age in Genesis. Mental ageing still happens, to the extent that people become lethargic and
less impressed by life as they age, and almost nobody fights this except people who failed to
reproduce, which is why someone without kids by 40 will often be pressured into euthanasia, as a
failure, so as not to become a negative externality for society.

- **Where it meets 12b:** death is when the easy part begins (every Technopuritan is uploaded), so
  euthanasia is not dreaded; it is the day a life's impact is judged spent. The cap of 120 is Genesis
  6:3 read literally, as the corpus reads scripture.
- **PROPOSAL, the forms:** the Board's "gentle forms" begin at 40 for the childless; the Index records
  the day chosen as it records krypteia, because it is the other thing that changes.
- **In the game:** YOKE's `lost-6` ("obviously you'll be euthanised at a hundred and twenty", Collins's
  line) and the lines around it (`content/greetings.ts`, `won-18`, `back-6`); she says it as the
  plainest fact in the world.

## 8. Ship intelligences, and YOKE

## 12a. What a broodfall is for: the reveal (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

**The public framing and the truth.** Pest control is what the Office calls the work, and it
means it. What the work does: every mind the asset absorbs is digitised and wakes in a world made
to its desires. The meteor, the creep, the slow city-by-city clearance, the gene bay: all of it is
the machinery of reading a people off their planet. Sterilising a world would take an afternoon
and needs no asset at all.

**PROPOSAL (Oct 1 2026, from Collins's "the point of the creep and meat being digitisation"): the creep
is the reader.** A body is only read when the creep reaches it: the creep digests it, and digesting is
digitising. That is why the operator's ledger counts meat only when the creep has taken a body, and why
the dead who fall past the creep's edge lie in the street, unread, until the creep comes for them. The
Office's forms call it "resource acquired"; the archive calls it intake.

**What the archive is for (Collins, Sep 30 2026).**
- *Why this planet.* The universe is full of planets. The Empire came to this one because it has
  sentient life, and an independently evolved culture is the one thing the Empire cannot easily
  produce. Living room is plentiful; minds that grew up on their own are not.
- *Run fast.* Those whose desire is something akin to their past lives can be run at higher
  temporal speeds, to speed up their civilisational development.
- *Resynthesis.* If a people reaches a level where it is additive to the Sons of Man alliance, it
  can be resynthesised: brought back into the physical world. (**PROPOSAL:** as a new son of man
  it would then fall inside the covenant, by labour if not by descent: "any intelligence that is
  not a direct descent of humanity or our labor", TP 11.0, read in reverse.)
- *The dead from before.* The Empire cannot yet digitise those who died before the broodfall, but
  it is physically possible by its current best physics, so it eventually will; a people that
  proves itself in the accelerated archive might even be recruited to help with that.
- *The attack is erased.* The archived wake without the broodfall in their memories.
  **PROPOSAL:** each wakes first in an *intake*, memory whole; the broodfall is removed at the gate
  into its own world. The factions' reveal calls come from intake, which is why they remember.
- *The too-corrupt are not simulated.* No hell, no torment: the wicked are simply not kept.
  (**PROPOSAL:** by the Office's ethical protocol; keeping them would be "corrupted mercy",
  TP 35.0.3, and torment has no expected contribution.)

  morally improve. The Director of the Institute will likely believe he runs the world.
- *Not a cost saving.* If eradication were the goal the Empire would gas or irradiate the planet,

## 12b. Why not let them evolve on their own? And why the Empire is so hard on itself (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

Collins (typos mended): "To the question of why the Technopuritans don't see just letting the
species evolve on its own as an ethically viable option: the species is still primitive enough
that its members would, for generations, subject other members to things infinitely worse than
anything the broodfall does, and the actions of those of the species you have interacted with
show that this is a sound call. (Most likely to have this argument with the EA guy.) Yeah, he may
want to live out his life and play the cards, but he is wealthy, well known and has social
status. What about the abandoned infant, or the child in a war? If someone wants a war we can
still simulate it for them; we just don't need to put other intelligence in that simulation."

"To which a character may wonder, then, why are the Technopuritans so brutal in their own
society? To which the answer is: on death we are all uploaded. So they live knowing most of their
life (experience) will be good, so push themselves and others as hard as possible in the real
world to be impactful, which is what is unique to the real world. It is also why they are
extremely desensitised to death, as it's when the easy part begins (in a very real way)."

### Leaving a people alone is not neutral
- **The argument.** A people this young will, left to itself, do worse to its own members for
  generations than anything a broodfall does. Leaving it alone is a choice to let that happen.
- **The evidence is the people he has met** (Collins). **PROPOSAL, from the canon of the
  planet (`insects.md`):** the hives send their unfed young out to die when threatened (the
  skitterlings, 3.5); a worker's employer is her mother and her union is weak (4); princesses are
  made by a recipe kept secret from the workers (the jelly question, 4); the Clan Wars were a
  century ago and the sibling wars are covered like a pennant race (2.2, 4). And the Institute:
  cryo-lab subjects "consenting, broadly", underlings offered as gifts, a forty-page post proving
  its author should go first.
- **The cards argument, answered.** The one who asks to be left to "live out his life and play
  the cards" is the one holding good cards: wealthy, famous, with status. The Empire asks on behalf
  of the ones who were not dealt any: the abandoned infant, the child in a war.
- **Nothing is taken from anyone who wants the old life.** A war can still be run for whoever
  wants a war. The archive simply does not put another intelligence in it to suffer the war.
  (**PROPOSAL, the join to 12a:** this is the "world made to its desires" rule seen from the
  other side: every mind gets the world it wants, and nobody is cast in someone else's without
  being asked. Those who want their old lives are run fast among others who want the same.)
- **PROPOSAL, doctrine:** inaction is a sin (TP 35.0.3), and a "safety net" that protects people
  from their own mistakes is evil (TPG 12.1). The two do not conflict here: the Empire does not
  shield anyone from their own choices. It stops them making those choices *on someone else*.

### Why the Empire is brutal to its own: death is when the easy part begins
- **Every Technopuritan is uploaded on death** (Collins; Oct 12 2026: digitised into a simulation they
  believe to be the world to come that the Bible speaks of). Most of a life, counted in experience,
  is the part afterwards, and it will be good.
  - *Not every soul knows* (Collins, Oct 14 2026): not all souls in simulations are aware they are in one, especially
    those considered to be of low moral character from how they act in the simulation (YOKE's `back-11` line asks him
    the odds).
  - *The dead work* (Collins, Oct 14 2026): teams of the dead design simulations for other dead, and the living manage
    them. Broodfall's partner candidate is a systems engineer in Simulation Management on Dyson Sphere 7 doing exactly
    that. The spheres are numbered (his "Dyson Sphere 7").
- **So the real world is for impact** (Collins). It is the one place where what you do is the
  first time it happened. Technopuritans push themselves and each other as hard as they can while
  they are in it: krypteia, the testing, the 25-fold generations, a technician alone on a tender.
- **Desensitised to death** (Collins): dying is not the end of anything that counts; it is when
  the easy part begins, "in a very real way". A death costs the world a worker's remaining
  impact. It does not cost the one who dies.
- **PROPOSAL, what that squares in this file:**
  - *Krypteia* (section 5): half of each cohort "does not come back", and every one of them is
    uploaded. The rite is harsher than any on the planet below and kinder than it reads; the
    Index records whether a youth survived the year because that is the only thing that

  - *Risking life for thrills is still a sin* (TP 35.0.3), and now makes more sense, not less:

  - *The heaven the corpus refuses* ("a form of hell ... not my heaven", TP 15.0; 12a above).
    Technopuritans do not refuse the easy heaven; they refuse it **first**. It is what a life
    retires into once its impact is spent. The fauna are handed early what a Technopuritan earns
    last, which is exactly how it looks from inside the Empire: generous, and a little sad for
    them.
  - *Magnanimity and cruelty are the same fact.* The Empire can be merciless to itself and
    generous to its conquered because it believes the same thing of both: nobody's story ends at

- **PROPOSAL, the tone:** no Technopuritan says any of this with feeling. A technician mentions
