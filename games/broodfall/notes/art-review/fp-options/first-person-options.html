Stay in first person the whole way?

Fourth version, built from your two notes: he walks to the table, picks up a visor and puts it on, and the ship's interface is its screen flickering on. First person from the pad to the interface, no staged hand, the window no longer changes, and every room stays as it is.

Decided October 4: the original film stays. The game keeps option A, and none of the films below went in. This page is kept as the record of what was tried.

D · The visorNew, October 4
  1. He lets go of the pad, looks right, and the desk and its window slide out of the picture.
  2. He stands and walks through the hatch. A black visor lies on the lit table.
  3. Both hands take it, turn it and raise it. Its inside closes over his eyes and the picture goes black.
  4. The screen flickers on: two dim flashes with a crackle, then it catches and steadies on the report.

The film runs 12.5 seconds to black, against 10 for today's. The interface at the end is a screenshot of the real game, held three seconds. The flicker and its sound are made by the tool, so in the game they would land on the frame.

Why the window changed

Each film left the desk with a clip that was told to end on a second, separately drawn picture of the same window. That second drawing had a thicker frame and a far redder planet, so the video model morphed one window into the other while he turned. All four of yesterday's first-person films open with that same clip, which is why it happened the same way in each. Today's in-game film does it too, in its walk.

One seam left

When the screen comes on, the Desk's backdrop still shows him from behind at the table, without the visor. Read as the visor's display it holds up: the ship's system showing him where he is. If it bothers you when you watch it, the fix is one picture: the Desk's backdrop redrawn with the visor's strap on his head.

What putting it in the game would have taken

  1. One more clip: the look-away for a lost mission, starting from the dropped pad. The walk and the visor are shared by both outcomes.
  2. The two films baked in place of today's, with the ship's sound bed.
  3. The handover changes from a fade to the flicker, because the film now ends on black instead of on the room. Reduce motion keeps its plain cross-fade, and Reduce flashes turns the flicker into a fade.
  4. The pad's tests and its four browser beats run again, then played by hand from a real mission's end.

About $1.30 of art and half a day. No room is redrawn.
