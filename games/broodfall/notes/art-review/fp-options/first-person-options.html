Broodfall · the pad, part 2 · from the desk into the ship

Stay in first person the whole way?

Yes, I think it looks better, provided it goes all the way: the film and every room of the ship seen through his eyes, with his hands in the picture. The half-way version, where he walks in first person and the camera pulls out as he arrives, is weaker than what the game does today.

To check that by eye, I drew four rooms the way he would see them, put the real interface over each one, and cut a test film of every option. All of it is below. Nothing in the game has changed.

Three ways to make the film

A · Today

The camera pulls out of his eyes while he sits at the desk. We watch him get up and walk to the table. The interface arrives with him standing at the left, seen from behind.

Nothing to build. 10 seconds.
B · Through his eyesMy pick

The camera never leaves him. He stands, turns past the window, walks through the hatch and lays his hand on the table. The report comes up over what he is looking at.

Eight rooms redrawn, one new walk clip. About $60 and a day. 10 seconds.
C · Settle back

First person for the walk. As he reaches the table the camera slides back out of his eyes and he comes into the picture from the left. It lands on today's interface.

Two clips, no room art. About $3 and an hour. 13 seconds.

These are test takes at 720p. The stand-and-turn that opens B and C is the clip from October 2 that was set aside when the film was restructured; the walks and the settle are new. The interface at the end of each film is a screenshot of the real game over that backdrop, held for three seconds, so the players read 13 and 16 seconds.

Where the film ends: the report at the desk

The report covers the right two thirds and YOKE's intercom takes the lower left corner. Today his back fills what is left. In first person that corner has to be earned, and it took three tries.

The third one also closes a loop. Part 1 of the film ends with his hand flat on the desk beside the pad. This ends with his hand flat on the table. For comparison, the report as it is today:

What goes away

He does. Today the man is in eight of the nine room backdrops, seen from behind, and his posture changes from room to room: bent over the notebook, seated at Comms, a hand in his pocket in front of YOKE. The Quarters is the one room already seen through his eyes.

In first person the player sees his hands, his forearms and the cord on his wrist, and nothing else of him outside the films. That reverses the note the rooms were drawn to on September 30, “the hero seen from behind in each room”. If seeing him aboard matters more to you than the unbroken viewpoint, stay with A.

Why not C

C keeps every room and costs almost nothing, so it is tempting. But it moves the change of viewpoint to the busiest second of the film: he arrives, the table wakes, the interface comes up and the camera leaves his eyes, all at once. A makes the change early, while he is sitting still, then runs one unbroken shot to the interface. If the rooms stay as they are, A is the better film.

If you pick B, the order of work

  1. The Desk first, looking down at the table with its light brought down, then the film's new ending for won and lost, and the handover from film to report checked in the game.
  2. AI Core, Comms and Gene Bay redrawn at full quality from these drafts, each with anything of him kept inside the left third.
  3. Locker, Licence, Directives and Notebook drawn the same way. The Quarters stays as it is.
  4. The loops: his hands rest and the room moves (the tank, the displays, the planet, YOKE's ring), with a slow drift of the whole picture so it breathes like a head, not a tripod.
  5. Today's room loops are kept beside the new ones, so one line switches the ship back.

Two things the test takes showed. The first two walks drew a window in the table room that is not there; describing the room's walls fixed it on the second take. And YOKE's greeting intercom covers the lower left of every room while she speaks, today and in first person alike, so his hands should sit above it.
