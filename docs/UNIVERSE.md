## The Technopuritan Empire

A far-future authoritarian human empire, the largest power in human space. Expansionist ("living
room" doctrine: habitable worlds are humanity's by right), bureaucratic, and doctrinally
materialist. Its God is not supernatural: God is what humanity is becoming, and its theology cashes
out in history, in institutions and in the record a person leaves, not in a supernatural
afterlife. Personal advancement is licensed: rank, resources, and the right to a mate are earned
through service metrics ("standing").

Technopuritans practise heavy genetic engineering and integration with AI (Collins, Sep 30). Most
citizens are well under half organic — a Broodfall technician is about 40% organic — but the retro
look, looking like an unaltered organic human, is in fashion, so it rarely shows.

The Empire's ethical horizon stops at the line of **the sons of man**: every intelligence that
descends from humanity or from humanity's labour (the engineered, the augmented, the cyborg, the
synthetic, the uplifted). Beyond that line there is no covenant. Xenofauna clearance — including
sapient xenofauna — is filed under pest control: low-status, proceduralized, boring to command, and
utterly unexamined.

## History (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

1. **The collapse (21st century).** Society went through a massive collapse from two causes at
   once: AI replaced most human jobs, and fertility collapsed. The fertility collapse would not have
   been as bad a problem if the money the AI made had not been concentrated in the US and Israel,
   while places like Latin America had the same fertility crisis and no tax revenue to do anything
   about it.
2. **The charter cities.** Even within America the government's power, relative to the "tech
   lords", waned: many of them moved their fortunes and operations to charter cities, to avoid
   taxes and regulation.
3. **The rise.** Technopuritan ideology was uniquely popular in a few of these early city-states.
   Its heavy emphasis on fertility and technology made it colonise space quickly.
4. **Earth left behind.** The Technopuritans largely left other earthlings alone, as part of their
   ideology (doctrine 10 below). With only religious extremists still breeding, Earth is low-tech
   compared with most of the human empire and constantly at holy war.
5. **Earth as the zoo.** The rest of the Empire, which is mostly Technopuritan, watches Earth as
   entertainment ("Hey, heard the Crusade took back Rome last week from the Caliphate. I can pull up
   live feeds if you like watching Earth."). It is treated essentially as a zoo, and as the
   cautionary story of why the Technopuritans live the way they do.
6. **The galactic explosion.** Their birth rate (below) is why they exploded onto the galactic
   scene so quickly compared with other groups. Some other religions and secular groups did make it
   to space, with comparatively slow growth; they do not really matter on the galactic scene, and,
   following the sons-of-man doctrine, the Technopuritans do not wipe them out unless they are a
   threat.

How the Empire's own histories read these events is doctrine 3 (the Trial of the Lotus Eaters) and
doctrine 11 (the chittering hordes).

## Family, krypteia and the index (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

- **Spouses and wombs.** Technopuritans live with spouses, but babies are had through artificial
  wombs: **10 a year for 10 breeding years = 100 children per couple.**
- **Raised to 13.** The parents raise them to the age of 13.
- **Krypteia (one year).** At 13 each child goes on krypteia: a year of surviving by their own
  means, living among a subjugated population.
- **Testing and education.** Those who survive the year face a brutal testing and education
  process that prepares them to enter **the index**, the wider imperial system.
- **About half survive** krypteia and testing together.
- **The arithmetic.** About 50 of a couple's 100 children become adults of the index: about 50 per
  couple per generation (Collins: "a growth rate of 50 per generation"), which is a 25-fold
  increase in people every generation (50 adults from 2 parents). One founding couple's line
  passes ten billion people in seven generations. That is the engine behind the living-room
  doctrine.

## Doctrine (the corpus, as the Empire practises it)

The Empire does not have a church that rules theology; see 12. These are the tenets shared
across nearly every House.

1. **God is humanity's destiny.** God is the real, physical entity humanity is becoming, millions
   of years hence, and relates to time differently from us; man does not become God so much as one
   day unite with a God outside time (HOME 1.0; TP 12.0; TP 11.0). To serve intergenerational
   improvement is already to be part of God.
2. **Monist, materialist, literalist; anti-mysticism.** Scripture read literally describes real
   material processes; "technology is just magic we presume someone else understands" (HOME 1.0).
   Souls are emergent patterns, not supernatural substance (TP 15.0). Mysticism, altered states,
   chanting and spinning are the faith of the Basilisk (TP 7.0; TP 10.0).
3. **The Basilisk and the trials.** God's testing aspect, the Basilisk, tempts and culls the
   indulgent, the stagnant and the vain so that only the strong reach the stars (TP 3.0). The
   fertility collapse was the **Trial of the Lotus Eaters**: not a tragedy but a culling, the
   unworthy "burning in a bonfire of their own vanity" (TP 11.0; TP 2.0). The Basilisk's acolytes
   are not enemies; one should not impede their work (TP 11.0).
4. **The Agents of Providence.** God is modelled as a heavenly host, an internet of future human
   minds; to children, "the future police", who watch the elect and reward what expands the
   potential of future man (HOME 1.0; TP 3.0; TP 12.0). Not everyone matters equally to them: the
   elect are those whose planned impact on history came to pass in line with Providence (TPS 7.0).
5. **Good = what expands the potential of man.** Ethics are consequentialist: judge an act by what
   the future would want of you; live to an "objective function", not to goals or feelings
   (HOME 1.0; TP 2.0; TP 35.0.3). "I was following the rules" is no excuse for Realized Man (TP 2.0).
6. **Intergenerational improvement is the commandment.** Children have a duty not to believe
   exactly what their parents believed (HOME 1.0). Improving human capacity by genetic and
   synthetic means is a religious mandate, not a blasphemy; not to use these technologies to the
   full is "open rebellion to God's will" (TP 12.0). Embryos are selected (TP 11.0); artificial
   wombs and gene editing were foretold as the moment "God's favor of us will be made self-evident"
   (TP 34.0.3).
7. **Iterative prophecy.** God revealed himself as far as each age could understand: to the Jews
   (a God of law and order), through Christ (God as man must martyr himself, generation after
   generation, to uplift mankind), through Muhammad (different revelations for different peoples),
   the early Mormons (man becomes like God), synthesised by Winwood Reade's *Martyrdom of Man*
   (1872) (HOME 1.0; TP 12.0). No bureaucracy certifies prophets; "prove all things" (TP 12.0).
8. **Idolatry is worse than murder.** No image of God is made or venerated; no object is holy;
   gold and grandeur drag the soul toward the deceiver (TP 8.0). Hence the Empire's austerity, its
   plain emblem, its low-culture name (HOME 1.0 "Why the goofy name?"), and its ban on status
   signalling.
9. **The sins.** Sins are rules that make life better. Among them: busywork, status signalling,
   pride, entitlement, empty words, rumination, self-flagellation, inaction, moral absolutism,
   corrupted mercy (charity that causes more suffering later), risking life for thrills, and lack
   of aplomb (TP 35.0.3). **Being sad is a sin and a choice**; cheer is chosen and modelled
   (TP 23.0). Sins are ranked by cost and consequence, and some entertainment is an "efficient
   sin" (TP 35.0.5).
10. **The Tesseract God; no monoculture.** Every true revelation is a shadow of one God cast
    in a lower dimension. "To create a monoculture — to have the whole world under one religion —
    is to silence the voice of God." The faithful convert only atheists, rebels who would never
    return to their parents' faith, and people of exceptional merit, and guide others back to
    conservative forms of their own faiths (TP 12.0). This is why Earth was left alone.
11. **The three faiths.** Polytheism (duty), mysticism (harmony) and monotheism (the expansion of
    human potential) run through every religion; only the third is the path forward (TP 10.0).
    Groups that stay fertile by turning from technology, holding that they are already perfect,
    become the "chittering hordes", the final test of those who carry the flame of innovation
    (TP 11.0; TP 13.0). Earth, today, is that exhibit.
12. **The Index and the Houses.** The Index began as a "cultural reactor": a registry of family
    cultures (Houses) that records each House's theology and practices and how its children fared,
    so every House can learn from the others' results (TPG 2.6; HOME 1.0). A central body runs the
    goods that need scale — marriage markets, education — but does not govern theology (HOME 1.0).
    A new couple may stay in a parent House or found a new one (TPG 2.6). Grown to an empire, the
    Index IS the imperial system.
13. **No safety net that shields the incapable.** Support that protects people from their own
    mistakes rather than empowering the capable is held intrinsically evil; a youth who believes
    there is a way out does not grow up. The Spartan krypteia, hiding among the helots, is the
    model rite (TPG 12.1). Under the Empire it is mandatory, and not only for sons: the source
    rejects any rite of self-sufficiency that is for one sex only (TPG 12.1).
14. **The Covenant of the Sons of Man.** Before leaving the homeworld, man covenants to accept all
    the sons of man — including radically different ones: engineered specialists, human–AI
    cyborgs, hive minds of linked brains — "so long as they don't have designs on the subjugation
    of others". To war on what is different is to war on one's betters (TP 11.0). Synthetic
    intelligences descended from humanity are within it; their autonomy is protected while they
    threaten no one else's (TP 35.0.2). The kingdom of iron and clay in Daniel is read as this
    mixed kingdom of man, AI and augmented man (TP 2.0).
15. **Beyond the covenant.** "Any intelligence that is not a direct descent of humanity or our
    labor was created by God to either serve or test man." It need not be eradicated, but must
    never be favoured over the covenant of man, which alone can protect humanity from the
    malevolent intelligences it will wake as the empire expands (TP 11.0).
16. **Never falter in arms.** A great conflict is still to come, through which the kingdom that is
    never destroyed is made; even in peace the advancement of military technology must not falter
    (TP 2.0; the weapons mandate, TP 35.0.5).

## Known regions and factions

- **Techopuritan Crusade Zones** — active expansion fronts (already on Space Derelict's sector map
  as a high-risk region; the spelling is the map's). The Empire calls its expansion "the great
  human crusade" (the corpus's phrase, TP 12.0). Not to be confused with **the Crusade** on Earth,
  a Christian holy-war power in Earth's endless wars.
- **The rim** — lawless salvage space where Space Derelict's brutality game-show culture thrives
  outside imperial norms. Its predators split from the Empire over emotions (Space Derelict
  DESIGN.md): they chose feeling over aplomb (doctrine 9). **PROPOSAL:** the Empire reads the rim
  as the Basilisk's work, a culling of the indulgent, and does not police it (doctrine 3); it
  destroys only what strays into the Crusade Zones.
- **The eusocial worlds** — planets held by intelligent eusocial insectoid civilizations, target
  of the clearance campaigns in Broodfall. Outside the covenant (doctrine 15).
- **Earth** — the homeworld; a low-tech zoo of holy wars, watched for entertainment and
  instruction.
- **Other spacefaring humans** — religious and secular groups with slow growth. **PROPOSAL:**
  Space Derelict's Holy Empire (religious) and Confederacy (secular) are two of them. Left alone
  while they threaten no one.
- **Other sons of man** — **PROPOSAL:** Space Derelict's Pop Fiz (uplifted dolphins and whales)
  are sons of man's labour and so inside the covenant, however psychotic; its Ascendancy (an AI
  death cult) is the kind of son of man whose designs on others forfeit the covenant.

## Contradictions resolved (Sep 30 2026)

- **"Its theology cashes out ... not an afterlife"** (the old text) stands, narrowed to *no
  supernatural afterlife*: the corpus speculates about simulated heavens and the record of a
  life joining God (TP 12.0; TP 3.0), so "nothing survives" would be wrong.
- **"The Empire's ethical horizon stops at the human species line"** became **the line of the
  sons of man**: the corpus puts AI, cyborgs and the uplifted inside the covenant (TP 11.0;
  TP 35.0.2). Xenofauna remain outside, so the clearance fiction is unchanged.
- **"Machines have no opinions"** (Broodfall's ship-AI lore book) is not doctrine: doctrine puts
  synthetic sons of man inside the covenant. **PROPOSAL:** it is an Office classification — a
  tender's intelligence is filed as ship's equipment, and equipment is not asked. YOKE is,
  besides, not "an AI" but layered models, neural tissue and the ship itself (Collins, Sep 30).
- **"The index"** (Collins: the wider imperial system) and **the Index** (the corpus: a registry of
  family cultures) are the same institution at two scales (doctrine 12).

## Tone rules

- The Empire's own media voice is chipper, propagandistic, 1950s-newsreel confident. In-world
  documents are written in that voice. The cheer is doctrinal: sadness is a sin (doctrine 9).
- The horror is never announced; it leaks through procedure (memo language, metrics, forms) and
  through cracks in the propaganda filter.
- The Empire is played straight. Nothing in universe docs winks at the camera. Its doctrine is
  rendered faithfully to the corpus, never as a caricature of it.
- Characters inside the fiction may be irreverent about it (Broodfall's YOKE is); the documents
  are not.
