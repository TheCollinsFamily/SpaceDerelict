## The Technopuritan Empire

A far-future authoritarian human empire, the largest power in human space. Expansionist ("living
room" doctrine: habitable worlds are humanity's by right), bureaucratic, and doctrinally
materialist. Its God is not supernatural: God is what humanity is becoming, and its theology cashes
out in history, in institutions and in the record a person leaves, not in a supernatural
afterlife. Personal advancement is licensed: rank, resources, and the right to a mate are earned
through service metrics ("standing").

Technopuritans practise heavy genetic engineering and integration with AI (Collins, Sep 30). Most
citizens are well under half organic — a Broodfall technician is about 40% organic — but the retro
look, looking like an unaltered organic human, is in fashion, so it rarely shows.

The Empire's ethical horizon stops at the line of **the sons of man**: every intelligence that
descends from humanity or from humanity's labour (the engineered, the augmented, the cyborg, the
synthetic, the uplifted). Beyond that line there is no covenant. Xenofauna clearance — including
sapient xenofauna — is filed under pest control: low-status, proceduralized, boring to command, and
utterly unexamined. (What a clearance actually does to the people it clears is the twist of
Broodfall: see "What a broodfall is for", below.)

## History (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

1. **The collapse (21st century).** Society went through a massive collapse from two causes at
   once: AI replaced most human jobs, and fertility collapsed. The fertility collapse would not have
   been as bad a problem if the money the AI made had not been concentrated in the US and Israel,
   while places like Latin America had the same fertility crisis and no tax revenue to do anything
   about it.
2. **The charter cities.** Even within America the government's power, relative to the "tech
   lords", waned: many of them moved their fortunes and operations to charter cities, to avoid
   taxes and regulation.
3. **The rise.** Technopuritan ideology was uniquely popular in a few of these early city-states.
   Its heavy emphasis on fertility and technology made it colonise space quickly.
4. **Earth left behind.** The Technopuritans largely left other earthlings alone, as part of their
   ideology (doctrine 10 below). With only religious extremists still breeding, Earth is low-tech
   compared with most of the human empire and constantly at holy war. It still has technology, just not
   the super-advanced kind. Dysgenics is real and was before the launch: only religious extremists have
   children, at different rates, and many peoples went extinct quickly (the Japanese, most of the secular
   West; Korea went strange instead: a cult that began as a scam gacha game, and chaebols breeding their
   staff toward a eusocial species, with idol-styled spokeswomen retired at twenty-five; the exodus after
   the North-South integration is where the Technopuritans' Koreans come from). The Technopuritans left because leaving was the easiest way to keep the
   Covenant of the Sons of Man given where society was going. Israel alone stays advanced: the Jews kept
   technology by genetically isolating their Orthodox majority (the Pharisees); half of them joined the
   Technopuritans (Jewish Houses in the Index), and the rest, the Sadducees, rebuilt the Davidic line with
   the Third Temple, gene-drove the Levites out of the other Jews, and run Israel's drone swarms, the
   satellite laser the feeds call "the goy beam", and the rest, as stewards of the Orthodox masses. The
   Empire calls that two peoples and a breach of the covenant; a brief war stayed small (the Empire could
   glass Israel, the Sadducees could not glass every sphere, and their many colonies would be trouble for
   centuries); the cold war now is that the Sadducees want the Caliphate wiped out and the Empire forbids
   it, so Israel grows every time it is hit and lives walled off (Collins, Oct 12 2026). The deeper quarrel
   (Collins, Oct 15 2026): the Sadducee temple makes the genetic decisions on behalf of its people, which to the Empire
   is eugenics, a council claiming to know better than God which genes are good, the highest sin; so Sadducee culture
   has no school that kills. The Sadducees, who hold a life precious in itself, call the Technopuritans kid killers, and
   do not understand the Empire's obsession with diversity or why it uplifted animals; the Technopuritans call them
   eugenicists who override the will of God out of lower-order fears. The Sadducees believe in no afterlife at all, as
   the historical Sadducees did not, do not upload, and find uploading the dead gruesome (Collins, Oct 15 2026). What
   no Technopuritan understands is a believer who fears death: the Pharisees, the Orthodox majority of Israel's surviving
   Jews under the stewards, believe in a world to come and fear it anyway, as do Earth's Christians (Broodfall's YOKE,
   `back-13`). The skirmishes were heightened when the Sadducees
   learned the Empire was uploading the Sadducees it killed, and demanded they be deleted; one of the uploaded did not
   want to be. The Empire said it was done and deleted only those who asked for it; were that discovered, the conflict
   would reignite (Collins, Oct 15 2026). The Sadducees keep no cap at a hundred and twenty: their ruling council is
   still ruled by the original man spliced from the Davidic line and the scientists who made him, and their families,
   many of them hundreds of years old, which is a large part of why the Pharisees see them as a kind of being genuinely
   set apart (Collins, Oct 15 2026). Who the real Jews are (Collins, Oct 26 2026): three groups claim it, the Technopuritan Jews (the Sons of Israel), the Sadducees and the Pharisees, and each of the three thinks it is the real one. When a Technopuritan says "Jews" they mean only the Technopuritan Jews. "The Pharisees" is the Empire's name for Israel's Orthodox majority (it was "the Satmar" until Oct 26 2026). The Sadducees' allies
   are America's Baptists, whom they supply, which keeps America peaceful and capable; the Mormons expand by
   police state, not fertility; the Anabaptists were mostly wiped out when law and order went, pacifists, and the
   Amish are extinct on Earth though not in space (below); Latin America emptied after
   China's collapse and America walled it off (Collins, Oct 12 2026). Inside the Empire the Technopuritan
   Jews are the Sons of Israel and the Mormons the Sons of Utah, each with its own structure and sphere.
5. **Earth as the zoo.** The rest of the Empire, which is mostly Technopuritan, watches Earth as
   entertainment ("Hey, heard the Crusade took back Rome last week from the Caliphate. I can pull up
   live feeds if you like watching Earth."). It is treated essentially as a zoo, and as the
   cautionary story of why the Technopuritans live the way they do.
6. **The galactic explosion.** Their birth rate (below) is why they exploded onto the galactic
   scene so quickly compared with other groups. Some other religions and secular groups did make it
   to space, with comparatively slow growth; they do not really matter on the galactic scene, and,
   following the sons-of-man doctrine, the Technopuritans do not wipe them out unless they are a
   threat.

How the Empire's own histories read these events is doctrine 3 (the Trial of the Lotus Eaters) and
doctrine 11 (the chittering hordes).

## Family, the school and the index (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

- **Spouses and wombs.** Technopuritans live with spouses, but babies are had through artificial
  wombs: **10 a year for 10 breeding years = 100 children per couple.**
- **Raised to 13.** The parents raise them to the age of 13, unless the Board judges the parents morally
  dubious or inefficient: then the Index raises them (Collins, Oct 12 2026; Broodfall's technician was).
- **Krypteia: removed** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): "the Technopuritans don't really have any groups to rule over, the way
  the lore has played out, and the schools serve the same brutalist function." Nobody to hide among; the school is
  the rite.
- **School.** At 13 the children enter school, which runs through what we would call a PhD: a brutal
  series of tests meant to measure fitness honestly, that prepares them to enter **the index**, the wider imperial
  system. Students kill students, and it counts for the killer.
- **About one in five survives** school (Collins, Oct 14 2026; earlier notes said half).
- **Nothing is forced** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): not the school, not giving the children to the Index, not the tanks
  (hence the cat people, who breed sexually). Moral failings are marked beside one's social id, pass to the next
  generation if bad enough, and decide what work one can get. Nobody starves: the society is wholly post-scarcity,
  and that is exactly what made people hungry for status and work, since work confers the one scarce thing, purpose.
- **Why nobody stops the killing** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): to suggest it implies one of two things, that you want the
  20th century's dysgenic crisis back (so stupid that nobody assumes it is your motive) or that you are a eugenicist. In
  the Empire everyone edits a child's genes at birth (not to would be selfish, and child abuse); a *eugenicist* is one
  who thinks they know, at an abstract level, which genes are good and bad for society, the word's real meaning. To a
  Technopuritan that is claiming to know better than God: if you refuse the dysgenic spiral and also refuse to let
  natural selection, death, play out in ordinary human interaction, some council must make those choices, and that is
  about the highest sin there is. The fears of dying, pain and suffering are held to be adaptations to another world,
  removed except where still relevant; to follow them, or let them rule you, is very bad. Sadducees hold a human life
  precious in itself; Technopuritans hold that it is the capacity to sacrifice oneself, which they believe Jesus's life
  taught, that gives a life its value (Broodfall's `content/lore/empire.md`, 6c). Editing temptation out of a child is
  scripture to them, read literally: if thy right eye offend thee, pluck it out; if thy hand, cut it off (Matthew
  5:29-30; Collins, Oct 15 2026). A weakness left in a child is a hand nobody cut off.
  So people very rarely break from the society; to them it all makes sense. Anyone who did decide not to live by
  the Index's rules would simply not stay in Technopuritan space: the rim, Space Derelict's setting, is the other
  post-scarcity society, without aspiration to morality or purpose, with constant blood sports, piracy and the like
  (Collins, Oct 14 2026).
  The Empire's reasons: without honest testing people would fake fitness and bring back the twentieth century's
  dysgenic crisis; otherwise the most genetically successful would simply be those who bred most, no measure of
  use to God; and it kills nepotism, since taking out the students with higher-status parents is the higher
  status. With the dead coming back in simulation they see no cruelty in it; putting one's own moral purity before
  humanity's long-term thriving is the cruelty.
- **Ageing and the cap** (Collins, Oct 12 2026): nobody ages past 23 in looks (for men it is an aesthetic
  choice, usually the wife's). Living forever would be trivial and would stagnate society, so people are
  euthanised when their contribution is over, when society has moved past their frame of reference, or at
  a strict cap of 120 years (God's cap in Genesis); mental ageing still happens, and the childless at 40
  are pressured into it, so as not to become a negative externality.
- **The arithmetic.** About 50 of a couple's 100 children become adults of the index: about 50 per
  couple per generation (Collins: "a growth rate of 50 per generation"), which is a 25-fold
  increase in people every generation (50 adults from 2 parents). One founding couple's line
  passes ten billion people in seven generations. That is the engine behind the living-room
  doctrine.

## Culture: disgust with the body (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

- A persistent part of Technopuritan culture is **disgust with the human body**, and with things
  like **sex, as offensively inefficient**. Reproduction happens in wombs; marriage is a working
  partnership, and the Empire finds it strange that early humans used their breeding partners
  "for sexual release and companionship".
- **Arousal** (Collins, Oct 26 2026): Technopuritans are engineered not to feel it (it leads to indecency and wastes
  time, which is why they are so confused that anyone would want to be sexual). It came back through natural evolution,
  tied to extreme aggression and violence, because of the school and the behaviour it rewards. They are generally
  embarrassed about it, and will not engineer it out, because it was shown to help true fitness in the system they set up.
- They regularly **look back with horror on things normal in our time**: jobs, dating, and the
  rest. The 21st century is taught the way we teach medieval medicine (Broodfall's
  `empire.md`, section 14, has the syllabus).
- Where it touches the corpus: the Empire's favourite prophet already called humans "poor
  savages ... eating flesh and blood, dwelling in vile bodies which degrade us every day to a level
  with the beasts" (Winwood Reade, quoted in TP 2.0); the corpus separates pleasure from
  procreation (TP 32.0); comfort breeds stagnation, "the greatest of all sins" (TP 11.0);
  busywork is a sin worse than indulgence (TP 35.0.3); enhancing the body is a mandate
  (TP 12.0). The disgust itself, as a mass cultural reflex, is Collins's; the corpus does not
  say the body is disgusting, and the Empire's doctrine does not require the reflex.

## Doctrine (the corpus, as the Empire practises it)

The Empire does not have a church that rules theology; see 12. These are the tenets shared
across nearly every House.

1. **God is humanity's destiny.** God is the real, physical entity humanity is becoming, millions
   of years hence, and relates to time differently from us; man does not become God so much as one
   day unite with a God outside time (HOME 1.0; TP 12.0; TP 11.0). To serve intergenerational
   improvement is already to be part of God.
2. **Monist, materialist, literalist; anti-mysticism.** Scripture read literally describes real
   material processes; "technology is just magic we presume someone else understands" (HOME 1.0).
   Souls are emergent patterns, not supernatural substance (TP 15.0). Mysticism, altered states,
   chanting and spinning are the faith of the Basilisk (TP 7.0; TP 10.0).
3. **The Basilisk and the trials.** God's testing aspect, the Basilisk, tempts and culls the
   indulgent, the stagnant and the vain so that only the strong reach the stars (TP 3.0). The
   fertility collapse was the **Trial of the Lotus Eaters**: not a tragedy but a culling, the
   unworthy "burning in a bonfire of their own vanity" (TP 11.0; TP 2.0). The Basilisk's acolytes
   are not enemies; one should not impede their work (TP 11.0).
4. **The Agents of Providence.** God is modelled as a heavenly host, an internet of future human
   minds; to children, "the future police", who watch the elect and reward what expands the
   potential of future man (HOME 1.0; TP 3.0; TP 12.0). Not everyone matters equally to them: the
   elect are those whose planned impact on history came to pass in line with Providence (TPS 7.0).
5. **Good = what expands the potential of man.** Ethics are consequentialist: judge an act by what
   the future would want of you; live to an "objective function", not to goals or feelings
   (HOME 1.0; TP 2.0; TP 35.0.3). "I was following the rules" is no excuse for Realized Man (TP 2.0).
6. **Intergenerational improvement is the commandment.** Children have a duty not to believe
   exactly what their parents believed (HOME 1.0). Improving human capacity by genetic and
   synthetic means is a religious mandate, not a blasphemy; not to use these technologies to the
   full is "open rebellion to God's will" (TP 12.0). Embryos are selected (TP 11.0); artificial
   wombs and gene editing were foretold as the moment "God's favor of us will be made self-evident"
   (TP 34.0.3).
7. **Iterative prophecy.** God revealed himself as far as each age could understand: to the Jews
   (a God of law and order), through Christ (God as man must martyr himself, generation after
   generation, to uplift mankind), through Muhammad (different revelations for different peoples),
   the early Mormons (man becomes like God), synthesised by Winwood Reade's *Martyrdom of Man*
   (1872) (HOME 1.0; TP 12.0). No bureaucracy certifies prophets; "prove all things" (TP 12.0).
8. **Idolatry is worse than murder.** No image of God is made or venerated; no object is holy;
   gold and grandeur drag the soul toward the deceiver (TP 8.0). Hence the Empire's austerity, its
   plain emblem, its low-culture name (HOME 1.0 "Why the goofy name?"), and its ban on status
   signalling.
9. **The sins.** Sins are rules that make life better. Among them: busywork, status signalling,
   pride, entitlement, empty words, rumination, self-flagellation, inaction, moral absolutism,
   corrupted mercy (charity that causes more suffering later), risking life for thrills, and lack
   of aplomb (TP 35.0.3). **Being sad is a sin and a choice**; cheer is chosen and modelled
   (TP 23.0). Sins are ranked by cost and consequence, and some entertainment is an "efficient
   sin" (TP 35.0.5).
10. **The Tesseract God; no monoculture.** Every true revelation is a shadow of one God cast
    in a lower dimension. "To create a monoculture — to have the whole world under one religion —
    is to silence the voice of God." The faithful convert only atheists, rebels who would never
    return to their parents' faith, and people of exceptional merit, and guide others back to
    conservative forms of their own faiths (TP 12.0). This is why Earth was left alone.
11. **The three faiths.** Polytheism (duty), mysticism (harmony) and monotheism (the expansion of
    human potential) run through every religion; only the third is the path forward (TP 10.0).
    Groups that stay fertile by turning from technology, holding that they are already perfect,
    become the "chittering hordes", the final test of those who carry the flame of innovation
    (TP 11.0; TP 13.0). Earth, today, is that exhibit.
12. **The Index and the Houses.** The Index began as a "cultural reactor": a registry of family
    cultures (Houses) that records each House's theology and practices and how its children fared,
    so every House can learn from the others' results (TPG 2.6; HOME 1.0). A central body runs the
    goods that need scale — marriage markets, education — but does not govern theology (HOME 1.0).
    A new couple may stay in a parent House or found a new one (TPG 2.6). Grown to an empire, the
    Index IS the imperial system.
13. **No safety net that shields the incapable.** Support that protects people from their own
    mistakes rather than empowering the capable is held intrinsically evil; a youth who believes
    there is a way out does not grow up. The Spartan krypteia, hiding among the helots, is the
    model rite (TPG 12.1). The Empire has no helots (Collins, Oct 15 2026), so its school is the rite, and not only for sons: the source
    rejects any rite of self-sufficiency that is for one sex only (TPG 12.1).
14. **The Covenant of the Sons of Man.** Before leaving the homeworld, man covenants to accept all
    the sons of man — including radically different ones: engineered specialists, human–AI
    cyborgs, hive minds of linked brains — "so long as they don't have designs on the subjugation
    of others". To war on what is different is to war on one's betters (TP 11.0). Synthetic
    intelligences descended from humanity are within it; their autonomy is protected while they
    threaten no one else's (TP 35.0.2). The kingdom of iron and clay in Daniel is read as this
    mixed kingdom of man, AI and augmented man (TP 2.0).
15. **Beyond the covenant.** "Any intelligence that is not a direct descent of humanity or our
    labor was created by God to either serve or test man." It need not be eradicated, but must
    never be favoured over the covenant of man, which alone can protect humanity from the
    malevolent intelligences it will wake as the empire expands (TP 11.0).
16. **Never falter in arms.** A great conflict is still to come, through which the kingdom that is
    never destroyed is made; even in peace the advancement of military technology must not falter
    (TP 2.0; the weapons mandate, TP 35.0.5).

## The Sons of Man (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

The Technopuritans uplifted a number of other Earth species. Collectively, humans and the uplifts
are **the Sons of Man**, and the covenant (doctrine 14) covers them all; the corpus already names
"uplifted intelligences" among the protected (TP 35.0.2). The corpus's covenant also takes in
synthetic minds and cyborgs (TP 11.0); Collins's definition names humans and uplifts, and the two
are read together. Uplifts look anthro, like the nekos (Collins).

- **The Sabbatean Skirmishes** (Collins, Oct 14 2026, by name only): a conflict the Empire fought; a Broodfall
  partner candidate's relative was a war hero of it. When, against whom and why are open.
- **Ascending early** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): most people without a partner by forty are pushed to ascend early,
  which is to say to end themselves and be uploaded before their time.
- **Same-sex attraction** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): generally dealt with through hormonal treatment and genetic
  modification; a part that resists is rare, and the person may judge themselves morally compromised by it. "The meat
  world" is their slang for the living world, as against the simulations of the dead.
- **The simulated dead** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): not every soul in a simulation knows it is in one, especially those
  judged of low moral character by how they act inside it.
- **The sanctified** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): from the scripture that some in heaven come to rule over angels; the most
  moral of the dead, proven in the simulations that make up heaven, operate as the minds of cities and large ships.
  Processing is in no short supply; intelligences of great moral character are, and finding them is part of what
  heaven is designed to do.
- **The dead speak only when called** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): once dead you do not interact with the living world unless
  the living call to you or ask for an open communication line (Broodfall's technician and his father; Atlas and her
  dead friend).
- **The fears, engineered away** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): the fear of dying, of pain and of suffering helped ancestors
  have more surviving offspring in another environment, and are removed except where still relevant; to be ruled by
  them is very bad.
- **Companions** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): most Technopuritans have a companion intelligence, as Broodfall's technician
  has YOKE: a synthetic intelligence made partially from the person's own nervous tissue. Taking a dead person as
  one's companion instead is possible and thought pretty weird. Oct 15 2026: taking a *sanctified* one (below) has
  become popular; having a novel synthetic built, as the technician did, is now the less common choice.
- **Bodies by world** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): humans optimised for high-gravity planets are nothing like those
  optimised for life in zero G (Broodfall's technician is zero-G optimised). The Empire kills over belief and
  morality, never over genes, holds no animosity over genetic difference, and finds Earth's ethnic wars strange.
- **The rainbow people** (Collins, Oct 26 2026; Broodfall's Delegation films): the Technopuritan history books' name
  for the culture that ruled most of the old world before the Migration, after the rainbow flag it gathered under. It
  held that society is improved by making everyone equal and treating everyone the same, which the Empire finds one of
  the oddest chapters of human history: if everyone were the same there would be no point to other people or to being
  an individual, and if they are not the same, treating them as equal puts the artist in charge and the leader at the
  easel. They ruled the only planet humans were on, then within one generation stopped breeding and let themselves be
  replaced (and imported people who said openly that they wanted them gone). The wording is the history books', not a
  neutral one.
- **The Migration** (Collins, Oct 26 2026): man leaving Earth to take his manifest destiny among the stars. Before it
  the peoples that became the Houses were called ethnic groups, and in much of the old world it was offensive to notice,
  talk about or research the differences between them (the fear was that knowing would lead to killing, or to favouring
  one's own). The Empire finds that backwards: each House takes pride in what makes it distinct.
- **Favouring one's own, and the Hologs** (Collins, Oct 26 2026): "animosity and prejudice are two totally different
  things." Most Houses favour their own, because that is evolutionarily beneficial; that is neither animosity nor a belief
  that one's own are objectively better. No House thinks it is in a place to judge that: it can only be determined
  through competition (the Empire's own philosophy: God favours the group that makes the right choice by letting it
  out-produce or outwit the others). Since everyone leans toward their own, someone near the central curve of human
  variation finds allies easily and allies with others naturally, which is why humans still look fairly human; a people
  far off the curve is everyone's outgroup, and the others team up against it.
  **Why Earth's racism confuses them** (Collins, Oct 26 2026): favouring your own group is so natural a part of everyday
  life that it is strange to imagine it not existing, and at the same time wanting there to be ONLY your group is just as
  bizarre. It is like a sport: you love it, you have your team and you grr at every other team; but if someone
  suggested every other team be eradicated, or that teams not be allowed to compete, or that one compete with a
  handicap, you would look at them as if they had grown a second head, because then the sport that makes you love your
  team would not exist. The Houses compete; nobody wants the competition gone or rigged. The Hologs are the lesson: a House that used genetics
  without restraint, aiming wholly at intelligence and efficiency, and became giant floating brains the size of
  spaceships. Its founder's hypothesis about fitness was wildly wrong: with the computing and the organic and synthetic
  companions every Technopuritan already has, more intelligence was only marginally useful, and it put a target on their
  backs. Religion backs it up: even when nobody can say why, God gave man these bodies for a reason, and changing them too
  much tends to turn out strictly less efficient (the people who engineered out defecation and urination needed a
  blood-filtering system every night instead).
- **Houses by ancestry** (Collins, Oct 26 2026): peoples keep to Houses by ancestry (the Sons of Korea, the Sons of
  Igbo, the Sons of Israel, the Sons of Utah) so the sons of man stay distinct. Marrying across two needs approval and
  both Houses read it as genetic dilution; the children may have to found their own House or join a looser one (the
  nekos'). Mixing was normal on old Earth until the Mesoamerican ethnic group mixed into extinction. Keeping lines
  distinct is favouring one's own, not animosity, and not a claim to be better.
  **Why mixed children have it so hard** (Collins, Oct 26 2026): this is a world after the Gene War (below), where wars
  over genetic mixture were fought at the level of the genes themselves, down to junk DNA. Each House hones variants of
  its own ancestors' tradition and competes them against the others', to make the best of what its ancestors were. Say
  the Sons of Igbo take in a mixed child: Korean genes now dilute the Igbo line, which is a strict win for the Sons of
  Korea, unless they are exceptionally strong Korean genes, in which case the Igbo are "stealing" them from the Koreans.
  Either way one House loses, so neither wants the child. And would a Korean who married in really adopt the House's
  ways and traditions, or impart their own?
- **The Lipptors, a human class, not an uplift** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): Technopuritans genetically specialised
  for advanced mathematics and extensive AI integration, so far that their organs begin to shut down if they are
  not integrated with an AI within a few days of birth. Genetically further from the rest of the Empire than
  Earth's humans are, and nobody calls them a subspecies; the uplifted are thought of differently.
- **Dogs.** Uplifted dogs rose into many high-level positions in the imperial bureaucracy: they
  like taking orders, are generally happy and trustworthy, and are generally thought more generous
  and nicer to be around than humans. Having one as a superior is considered quite the gift. They
  rarely hold top management, because they are very gullible. They are the most common uplifted
  species in the Empire and were the first, though only a few breeds were uplifted; breeds like pit
  bulls were eradicated. Overly generous and often unable to make the ruthless decision, they are for
  that very reason near-universally trusted in middle management and elected to office at unusually
  high rates (Collins, Oct 14 2026). (Broodfall: the player's boss is an uplifted dog.)
  **Dogs kill the fewest at school** (Collins, Oct 18 2026). In anyone else that would bring suspicion; dogs are given a
  pass, as part of the "optimisation": the uplifts were made to add a diversity of cognitive perspectives to the Empire,
  and a mind that optimises for compassion is thought "safe" in a dog, because you know that "prejudice" the moment you
  see one, unlike a human who is overly sentimental. Sentimentality is feared because it primes a person for
  deontological moral framings, one of the worst sins a Technopuritan knows: it lets you feel pure and good while you
  pass the cost to others (refuse to shoot one person to save fifty and you have handed the cost of your purity to the
  fifty), and an interstellar empire's costs are on another scale. So although dogs reach power at unusual rates, even
  they know not to make the final call, and they generally distrust their own moral instincts.
- **Elephants.** Top management is usually held by humans or uplifted elephants.
- **Dolphins and killer whales.** Generally thought to have been a massive mistake: they border on
  a "genetically evil" race, and mostly live on the fringes of the Empire. Space Derelict's **Pop
  Fiz** are them: manic, "murder-happy", fond of reminding people that 21st-century orcas tossed
  baby seals in the air for fun (Space Derelict `space_derelict/model.py`).
- **Orcas' reputation** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): by far the worst of the uplifts: smarter than humans and able to trick
  them easily; where Technopuritans kill to show status or achievement, orcas kill for fun, torturing their victims. The
  last orca admitted to a Technopuritan school left twelve tortured bodies by the end of year one, some too
  rotten to upload.
- **The rim's uplifts** (Collins, Oct 14 2026): every uplifted species on the rim descends from animals uplifted in
  the early days of human space travel, while Earth still had a diverse ecosystem. They are on the rim because their
  species built a bad reputation in the Empire or could not live by Technopuritan values, like the dolphins and orcas.
- **The rim's gladiatorial stations** (Collins, Oct 14 2026, through Broodfall's Rook): fighters bred for
  entertainment, part orca for the aggression; a winner gets freedom and money enough for a ship. A convert from the
  rim becomes a full Technopuritan only by acceptance into a House, which requires marriage; the Empire rates a
  ring girl low status, and she answers that nobody out of its hardest school is as provably fit.
- **Why there are uplifts at all** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): "the reason the various animals were uplifted in the first
  place was to increase the diversity of intelligent entities in the Empire. In an empire that believes God speaks
  through better iterations out-competing worse ones, to lessen diversity is to silence God" (doctrine 10; "all require
  diversity", TP 11.0). An uplift is another kind of mind for God to speak through, not labour and not a pet.
- **The Gene War and the womb clause** (Collins, Oct 15 2026): in the Empire's early days an arms race of gene drives
  and similar technology, on the idea that "the strongest genetic profile wins" means just that: ever more aggressive
  strategies that worked only at the genetic level, to get more genes like yours replicated. Severe dysgenic effects
  followed, since the genes best at winning the gene war were not necessarily good at interpersonal or inter-community
  competition. The Index banned the practice as a violation of the covenant. Almost every House and faction agreed,
  except the nekos, for whom breeding true is the most distinctive thing about their
  sub-species; to drop it would be to become like everyone else, which to a Technopuritan is an existential loss (the
  bullet above). So the ban was written to cover only the artificial making of children and their gestation in tanks,
  an exception written in for them: they breed organically, editing their blastocysts with nanites (they are not
  animals), but in utero, which keeps the covenant. The Index accepted it because it prevents the runaway in their
  numbers that breeding true in tanks would bring. The nekos do not use the tanks because it is illegal: the one
  people who kept the covenant the hard way, orthodox and vulgar at once. Long form, with what is PROPOSAL:
  Broodfall's `content/lore/empire.md`, section 4b.
- **The nekos' own case** (Collins, Oct 14 2026, through Broodfall's Mirelle): they keep sexual reproduction as
  cultural preservation, not pleasure; were it wrong they would be extinct, so God approves. Against the tanks they
  are offset by breeding true, in litters of three or four, all female, with mothers raising their own until school.
  A neko without a mate in the Empire goes to the rim for one, easy and dangerous. Watched as outsiders, they
  keep the norms harder than most.
- **Cats** (Collins, Oct 18 2026). Cats were uplifted early, like the dogs, but they were one of the species, like the
  dolphins and orcas, that could not bring themselves to work within Technopuritan culture: they lacked self-control and
  liked breaking things for fun at too high a rate. Uplifted cats mostly live in the outer rim now.
- **Nekos.** The most unusual uplift: anthro-looking like the others, all female, and they breed
  true — a neko's daughter is a neko, taking little of the husband's DNA. They were
  created, essentially, because someone thought it would be hot if nekos existed, and in part out of frustration that one
  of humanity's longest partner species was not in the Empire: the nekos were engineered as a cat variant that could work
  within it (Collins, Oct 18 2026). Some live
  within Technopuritan society, but because they breed true they ironically struggle to find
  breeding partners, and often go to the fringes to find humans who still like sex for its own
  sake. They could engineer males, but refuse: it would "destroy their culture". They do not breed in tanks: it is
  illegal (the Gene War and the womb clause, above). Space Derelict's **Felonia** (prides of "catfolk" that approach crews wanting to "breed"
  and "value skill over carnage") are those fringe nekos.
- **Fringe politics (PROPOSAL):** the covenant protects every uplift from extermination, not from
  disdain. The Empire keeps the dolphins and orcas at arm's length on the fringe and does not ask
  what happens out there; the nekos come and go.

## The eusocial worlds (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

Planets held by intelligent eusocial insect civilisations: the targets of the Empire's clearance
campaigns, outside the covenant (doctrine 15), filed as tests and cleared as pest control. The one
known in detail is Broodfall's; its long form is `games/broodfall/content/lore/insects.md`.

- **Collins's canon for Broodfall's world:** a eusocial species in large clan groups, with an
  economy like Korea's chaebols, run by royalty. Only royals breed, and a queen lays hundreds of
  eggs. Most of the species is technically barren females, the worker caste. Males are rare
  (Broodfall's techbro, the Institute's Director, is one, and his partners are workers). One
  religion dominates the planet, and atheism has started becoming common.
- **Level:** 21st-century technology on the edge of AI (they have just built their first AI
  models), with the manners of 1950s America. The Empire watches them, as it watches Earth, as a
  parody mirror of old humanity.
- **The mirror nobody names:** one faith, breeding reserved to a few, and an order that believes
  itself complete: the corpus's "chittering hordes" (doctrine 11). The Empire, whose own
  citizens need a licence to breed, never makes the comparison.
- **PROPOSAL:** other eusocial worlds need not share this one's culture. What they share is
  biology (a laying royal line, a barren majority) and the Empire's filing of them.

## What a broodfall is for (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

Collins: "when someone is absorbed by one of the structures they are transported to an artificial
environment based on their desires (essentially an artificial heaven) ... this is the only reason
for the broodfalls anyway, with the character quite surprised that this was not obvious to
characters given how easy it would be for a civilisation with our level of technology to
eradicate all the life on a planet." And: "you believe you are playing a genocide simulator, but
all factions are actually significantly more 'right' about you than the player realises, and your
civilisation is significantly more magnanimous than it is originally framed."

- **What absorption is.** Whatever the asset absorbs is digitised: each mind wakes in an
  artificial world shaped to its own desires. A broodfall clears a planet of its bodies, not of
  its people. The Empire could sterilise a world in an afternoon; the meteor, the creep and the
  city-by-city pace exist because the asset is reading the people it takes (**PROPOSAL:** the
  reading is done as it digests; a bomb would read no one).
- **What the archive is for (Collins, Sep 30 2026).** The universe is full of planets; the Empire
  comes to a world like this one because it holds sentient life, and an independently evolved
  culture is the one thing the Empire cannot easily produce. Those whose desire is something akin
  to their past lives can be run at far higher temporal speeds, to speed up their civilisational
  development; if a people reaches a level where it is additive to the Sons of Man alliance, it
  can be **resynthesised**: brought back into the physical world. (So a broodfall is not a cost
  saving either. If eradication were the goal, gas or radiation would do it for a fraction of the
  price.)
- **The dead from before (Collins, Sep 30 2026).** Those who died before the broodfall are not in
  the archive: the Empire does not have the technology to digitise the dead YET, but it is
  physically possible by its current best physics, so it eventually will. A people that proves
  itself in the accelerated archive might even be recruited to help build it.
- **Memory, the wicked, and the line (Collins, Sep 30 2026).**
  - *The attack is erased.* The archived wake without the broodfall in their memories.
    **PROPOSAL, so the reveal scenes can happen:** each mind wakes first in an *intake*, with its
    memory whole; the broodfall is taken out when it passes through the gate into its own world.
    The factions' last calls to the character are made from intake.
  - *The too-corrupt are not kept.* People judged too morally corrupted are simply not simulated.
    There is no hell: nobody is tortured; the wicked just are not kept.
  - *The near-line are kept apart.* People near that line are kept in private simulations until
    they morally improve (Collins's example: the Institute's Director will likely believe he runs
    the world).
  - **PROPOSAL, the doctrine under it:** keeping the corrupt among the rest would be "corrupted
    mercy" (charity that causes more suffering later, TP 35.0.3); torturing them would serve no
    objective function (doctrine 5). The judging is done by the Office's ethical protocol, the
    same protocol that forbids shutting an archive down on request.
- **Why the Empire does it (the doctrine, faithfully):**
  - *Beyond the covenant, but not to be eradicated.* Intelligences not descended from man were made
    "to either serve or test man"; they "need not be eradicated", and must never be favoured over
    the covenant of man (TP 11.0; doctrine 15). The living room goes to man, and the minds that held
    it are not destroyed. Both halves of the tenet are kept at once.
  - *A soul is a pattern.* Souls are emergent patterns, not substance (TP 15.0); a pattern carried
    whole onto another substrate is the same soul.
  - *A simulated life weighs the same.* "The moral weight of actions and lives in that universe and
    universe prime would be equal as both are 'just' simulations — it is just that one is running on
    silicon" (TP 11.0). So an uploaded fauna has lost nothing the doctrine counts.
  - *The corpus saw the technology coming:* a lattice "that can port our likeness into either
    simulated heavens or hells and host us there for great lengths of time" (TP 12.0); heaven read
    as a time, the future, when the dead are brought back "through simulations or through literal
    resurrections" (TP 15.0; TP 1.2).
  - **PROPOSAL, the edge (amended Sep 30 2026: Technopuritans are uploaded on death too, see
    below, so this heaven is not refused, only taken last):** the corpus calls a heaven of
    pleasant experience in which one can no longer affect anything "a form of hell ... not my
    heaven" (TP 15.0). To the fauna who want only comfort, the Empire gives early the heaven it
    saves for after a life's work: comfort, with no hand on history. Those who want their old lives back get the one heaven the corpus would
    accept: work that matters, run fast, with a way back into the world (resynthesis, above). It is
    magnanimous, and it also takes a people out of the race for living room until it is fit to
    rejoin it on man's side. Nobody in the Empire finds that tension worth a sentence.
  - **PROPOSAL:** the Office's name for the destination is the **archive**; a technician can see
    it on a monitor, and can delete from it (Collins: "you can always just delete all their members
    from the simulation if they want"). What protocol stops him doing so casually is the same
    ethical protocol that stops a whole archive being shut down on request (Collins).
- **How it is hidden in plain sight.** Nobody hides it. Every imperial document is literally
  true: "clearance" (the planet is cleared), "specimens" (a specimen is kept), "absorb",
  "retrieval", "viability threshold: any". A technician knows it from training and finds it too
  obvious to mention, so his cheerful logs never do. The pest-control framing is what the Office
  calls the work, and the Office means it: to the Empire, moving a people off a planet into a
  heaven really is pest control.
- **What the planet believed.** The Faith's scripture foretold the sky cracking, the end and a
  heaven after, and it was accurate. **PROPOSAL, the Technopuritan reading:** God, outside time,
  reveals to each people what it can hold (doctrine 7), so God likely steered their Book toward
  what would really happen.

## Why not leave them alone; why the Empire is hard on itself (Collins, Sep 30 2026)

The long form, with Collins's words in full, is Broodfall's `content/lore/empire.md`, section 12b.

- **Leaving a primitive people to evolve on its own is not ethically viable.** Its members would,
  for generations, subject each other to things infinitely worse than anything a broodfall does,
  and the ones the character meets prove it. The one who asks to "live out his life and play the
  cards" is the one with good cards (wealth, fame, status); the Empire asks for the abandoned
  infant and the child in a war. Whoever wants a war can still have one simulated; the archive
  just does not put another intelligence in it.
- **On death every Technopuritan is uploaded.** Most of a life's experience is the part
  afterwards, and it will be good. So the real world, the one place where what you do is the first
  time it happened, is for impact: they push themselves and each other as hard as possible in it.
  That is why the Empire is brutal to its own (the school, the testing), and why it is extremely
  desensitised to death: death is "when the easy part begins, in a very real way".
- **PROPOSAL, consequences:** the school's dead are uploaded like anyone else; risking life for
  thrills stays a sin (death is cheap, real-world time is not); the easy heaven the corpus calls
  "not my heaven" (TP 15.0) is not refused, only taken last, after the work.
- **The dead keep in touch** (Collins, Sep 30 2026): the uploaded dead message the living. A dead
  parent can still nag about finding you a mate, and the fifth commandment (honour your father and
  mother) is honoured after death as before: the Ten Commandments matter to the society.
- **Open:** Earth is left in holy war (the zoo). Whether its dead are kept is Collins's call
  (`empire.md`, section 16).

## Contradictions resolved: the reveal (Sep 30 2026)

- **"The horror is never announced; it leaks through procedure"** (tone rules, below) stands, and
  now works as a double bluff. The Empire's voice is played straight and it is telling the truth;
  the horror the player reads into its procedure is real in what it says (a people's world is
  taken; the Empire would treat them as pests) and misleading in what it implies (that they die).
  The tone rules are unchanged; they gained a second layer.
- **"The Office has never filed a world under *serve*"** (Broodfall's `empire.md`, section 12)
  stays true. The unexamined line the lore book pointed at was examined long ago: *need not be
  eradicated* is exactly what the archive honours.
- **"Its theology cashes out ... not in a supernatural afterlife"** (above) stands: the archive is
  a technological afterlife, not a supernatural one, and the corpus expects such things (TP 12.0).

  as a high-risk region; the spelling is the map's). The Empire calls its expansion "the great

  DESIGN.md): they chose feeling over aplomb (doctrine 9). **PROPOSAL:** the Empire reads the rim

  destroys only what strays into the Crusade Zones.
- **The eusocial worlds** — planets held by intelligent eusocial insectoid civilizations, target
  of the clearance campaigns in Broodfall. Outside the covenant (doctrine 15). See "The eusocial

  instruction.

  small original community exploded in size. In daily life they use little technology; on Earth they are extinct.
  Inside the covenant, left alone.

  Space Derelict's Holy Empire (religious) and Confederacy (secular) are two of them. Left alone

## Contradictions resolved (Sep 30 2026)

- **"Its theology cashes out ... not an afterlife"** (the old text) stands, narrowed to *no
  supernatural afterlife*: the corpus speculates about simulated heavens and the record of a
  life joining God (TP 12.0; TP 3.0), so "nothing survives" would be wrong.
- **"The Empire's ethical horizon stops at the human species line"** became **the line of the
  sons of man**: the corpus puts AI, cyborgs and the uplifted inside the covenant (TP 11.0;
  TP 35.0.2). Xenofauna remain outside, so the clearance fiction is unchanged.
- **"Machines have no opinions"** (Broodfall's ship-AI lore book) is not doctrine: doctrine puts
  synthetic sons of man inside the covenant. **PROPOSAL:** it is an Office classification — a
  tender's intelligence is filed as ship's equipment, and equipment is not asked. YOKE is,
  besides, not "an AI" but layered models, neural tissue and the ship itself (Collins, Sep 30).
- **Felonia as "aliens".** Space Derelict's asset prompts call Felonia "sexy neko aliens"
  (`games/space-derelict/generate_event_assets.py`). Collins's Sep 30
  word is that nekos are an Earth uplift, so Felonia are uplifted nekos, not aliens. The
  prompts only describe pictures and were left unchanged; every other Space Derelict detail
  (prides, "breed" offers, skill over carnage, Pop Fiz's seal-tossing orcas) stands.
- **"The index"** (Collins: the wider imperial system) and **the Index** (the corpus: a registry of
  family cultures) are the same institution at two scales (doctrine 12).

## Tone rules

- The Empire's own media voice is chipper, propagandistic, 1950s-newsreel confident. In-world
  documents are written in that voice. The cheer is doctrinal: sadness is a sin (doctrine 9).
- The horror is never announced; it leaks through procedure (memo language, metrics, forms) and
  through cracks in the propaganda filter. Since the reveal (above, "What a broodfall is for") the
  procedure is also literally true: every word the Empire writes about a clearance must stay true
  of an upload, and nothing says outright that the absorbed live.
- The Empire is played straight. Nothing in universe docs winks at the camera. Its doctrine is
  rendered faithfully to the corpus, never as a caricature of it.
- Characters inside the fiction may be irreverent about it (Broodfall's YOKE is); the documents
  are not.
